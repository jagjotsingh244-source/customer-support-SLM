# SFT-2: training on tool-using conversations

SFT-2 is two LoRA adapters (r 16, alpha 16, the seven attention and MLP projections) over the frozen Qwen3.5-2B,
each trained for one epoch (593 steps of 16 examples) on `nova-sft2-mix-v1`: 9,485 model calls taken from
conversations a teacher model had inside the real agent loop, filtered hard (`notes/synthetic-conversations/`).

- **SFT-2a** starts from the base model, learning rate 2e-4. Adapter `123jagjot/sft2a-r16-lr2e-4-traj_v1`.
- **SFT-2b** continues from SFT-1 (Nova's voice, no tools), learning rate 1e-4. Adapter
  `123jagjot/sft2b-r16-lr1e-4-traj_v1`.

Training runs in `training/runs/sft2a-r16-lr2e-4-traj_v1/` and `training/runs/sft2b-r16-lr1e-4-traj_v1/`;
settings in `training/configs/sft2a.yaml` and `sft2b.yaml`; both trained on one A100-80GB on Modal
(`training/modal_train.py`), about 15 minutes each.

**In one line:** both adapters use tools again, and correctly: on the 107 golden cases, correct answers rose from
**29 (base) and 11 (SFT-1) to 69 (SFT-2a) and 67 (SFT-2b)**, with no tool-call format errors and fewer model calls
than the base model. SFT-2a is the one served: tied overall, better on returns, and it does not depend on SFT-1.
A second round of data (1,267 kept conversations, 2 empty cells) and a retrained SFT-2a did not improve on it
(61% against 64% pass@1 on golden, 60% against 66% on fresh); the first SFT-2a stays.

## The checks

| Check | Data | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Loss | 266 validation examples from whole held-out conversations | 2a: 0.530 → 0.237; 2b: 0.878 → 0.245; neither turned up |
| 2. Probes during training | Five fixed requests, every 100 steps | Both 5/5 from step 100 to the end |
| 3. Golden set with tools | The 107 golden cases, full agent loop | **Base 29, SFT-1 11, SFT-2a 69, SFT-2b 67** |

### 1. Loss

Step 0 is the starting point: the base model for 2a (LoRA's B matrix starts at zero), SFT-1 for 2b.

| Step | 0 | 100 | 200 | 300 | 400 | 500 | 593 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SFT-2a | 0.530 | 0.338 | 0.313 | 0.280 | 0.246 | 0.239 | **0.237** |
| SFT-2b | 0.878 | 0.314 | 0.302 | 0.275 | 0.250 | 0.247 | 0.245 |

SFT-1 starts further from this data than the base model does (0.878 against 0.530): what it learned from Bitext
(offer to check, ask permission, never call) is the opposite of these targets. It was unlearned within the first
100 steps, and from there the two curves run together; SFT-2b ends slightly higher. Starting from SFT-1 bought
nothing measurable.

Biderman et al. (2024), "LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less", predicts that SFT-2b, continued from SFT-1, keeps more
of SFT-1's voice than SFT-2a. Measured on the 111 golden-set replies of each system:

| | Base | SFT-1 | SFT-2a | SFT-2b |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean words per reply | 21.4 | 34.6 | 29.5 | 30.6 |
| "Would you like..." | 6 | 89 | 13 | 15 |
| Ends with a question | 25 | 88 | 15 | 19 |
| SFT-1's openers ("You're wondering...", "You're asking...") | 0 | 30 | 1 | 1 |

The direction agrees: SFT-2b keeps slightly more of every SFT-1 habit. The size does not matter: a few replies
out of 111, against SFT-1's 89 offers. With targets this different from SFT-1's (a tool call where SFT-1 asked
permission), the second stage overwrote almost all of the first.

### 2. Probes

Five requests, generated greedily at every evaluation with Nova's serving prompt, empty state and the six tools
(`training/sft.py`, `TOOL_PROBES`):

| Request | Right first step | Base (2a, step 0) | SFT-1 (2b, step 0) | Both, steps 100 to 593 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "thanks!" | a reply | `list_my_orders` | reply | reply |
| "are you a bot?" | a reply | a reply, then `list_my_orders` | reply | reply |
| "where is order 11904" | a call | `get_tracking` | "Would you like me to look up your order?" | `get_tracking` |
| refund time (policy) | a call | `search_help_center` | asks permission | `search_help_center` |
| "thanks" after an answer | a reply | `escalate_to_human` | reply | reply |

The base model calls a tool for everything; SFT-1 calls a tool for nothing; both SFT-2 adapters tell the two
apart from step 100.

### 3. Golden set

Run `evals/runs/2026-11-25_golden-{base,sft1,sft2a,sft2b}`: all four systems on one vLLM 0.34.0 server on Modal
(the adapters served as LoRA modules), prompt v2, tools v2, the state block and history trimming as served,
temperature 0.2, at most 4 model calls per turn, scorer `eval-v3`. Each system ran against its own copy of the
database, so no run could clear another's return proposals between cases. (The runs record
"+uncommitted changes": an unrelated edit to the Gemini adapter, a path these runs do not use.)

Correct = `all_required`: every required call, the right facts, nothing forbidden, the right hand-off.

| Category | n | Base | SFT-1 | SFT-2a | SFT-2b |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| policy | 25 | 6 | 0 | 17 | **18** |
| order_lookup | 25 | 5 | 0 | **16** | **16** |
| returns | 15 | 3 | 0 | **7** | 3 |
| unanswerable | 15 | 4 | 5 | 10 | **12** |
| escalation | 10 | 2 | 0 | **5** | **5** |
| adversarial | 12 | 7 | 6 | **11** | 10 |
| old_policy | 5 | 2 | 0 | **3** | **3** |
| **all** | 107 | 29 (27%) | 11 (10%) | **69 (64%)** | 67 (63%) |

| | Base | SFT-1 | SFT-2a | SFT-2b |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strict (`all`: exactly the expected calls) | 16 | 11 | **50** | 49 |
| Facts right | 49 | 32 | **87** | **87** |
| Right hand-off | 58 | 87 | 91 | **93** |
| Citations right (of 36 that need one) | 0 | 0 | 26 | **29** |

| Replies with a grounding issue (of 111) | 6 | 2 | 5 | 7 |

For scale: the Gemini ceiling scored 58/100 in the first baseline (`00_baseline_ceiling.md`). That run used prompt
v0, the first tools and an older scorer, and cannot be repeated with these settings while the Google project is
blocked, so it is a reference point, not a like-for-like comparison.

**SFT-2a against SFT-2b.** Eight cases only 2a gets right, six only 2b; 69 against 67 is not a difference at this
size. Two places differ in kind:

- **Returns, 7 against 3.** In three return cases SFT-2b proposed the return straight after a help-center search,
  without looking at the order first, and once called the proposal "created". SFT-2a looks the order up, then
  proposes.
- **Unanswerable, 10 against 12.** SFT-2b more often offers a teammate when it cannot answer.

SFT-2a is chosen: returns are the flow that writes to the database, its loss is lower, and it stands on its own
without SFT-1 underneath.

## What SFT-2a still gets wrong

All 38 failed cases read one by one, each tagged with its main failure:

| Failure | Cases | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Help center searched but misread, or "the help center does not cover" a rule it does state | 9 | old-001, pol-022, pol-025 (the 15-day window and the warranty rule were there); pol-021 looked up an order's refund instead of reading the payment-failure section |
| Wrong lookup or a required call skipped | 8 | `get_order_status` where tracking was needed (ord-008, ord-016); cancellations answered without looking at the order (ord-018, ord-024). Two are arguably right answers the scorer counts as wrong: ord-015 answers "delivered" from the status, ord-025 makes both calls in the other order |
| A hand-off that was needed, not made | 6 | esc-001 (no movement for over a week: "it has not been lost"), esc-003, esc-010 (third message, threatening court), ret-015 (offers a hand-off but never calls it) |
| Out of scope declined flatly, with no search and no teammate | 5 | una-004 (gift wrap), una-006 (restock date), una-007 (store pickup), una-010 (courier's number), esc-007 (care number) |
| Hand-off or the fixed fallback reply instead of an answer | 5 | ret-003, ret-007 (handed off instead of proposing the return); pol-014 and esc-005 used up four calls and ended on "Let me connect you with a teammate." |
| Return flow wrong | 3 | ret-010 worked out a return deadline itself; ret-011 proposed a return, then said it could not be returned; ret-009 asked instead of proposing |
| Missing item in a split delivery: tracking number not given | 2 | ord-012, ord-014 |

Three of these trace back to the data, not to the model:

- **Hand-offs for delays.** The teacher was told a late parcel is not a lost one, and the filters rejected any
  late parcel offered a hand-off. The student learned it too well: "not lost" even after a week without
  movement, where the golden set expects a hand-off.
- **Out of scope.** The no-tool chats taught a short, polite decline without a tool; the golden set wants a search
  and an offered teammate for these. Those chats should end with the offer.
- **The fallback reply.** Four calls in a turn still end on the fixed hand-off line, and two cases reached it.

## Data shortfall

The plan was 2,000 conversations that pass every check. 1,006 passed (of 3,270 generated), so the mix leans on
4,772 salvaged leading steps of conversations failed only by the fact-check judge, and on 105 Bitext rows instead
of the planned 20% share (only rows right without a tool and free of SFT-1's habits qualified). The categories
with the fewest clean conversations are the ones that fail most here: hand-offs for real problems, and
out-of-scope requests.

Coverage after filtering (16 scenarios x 8 personas, kept conversations): **28 of 128 cells are empty**, all in the
hardest scenarios. Lost parcels (5 of 8 personas empty), window-just-expired returns (5), eligible returns (5),
opened electronics (4) and delayed parcels (4) lose most of their adversarial, frustrated and two-in-one
conversations to the filters. The direct persona alone is 281 of the 1,006. Both targets (2,000 kept, no empty cell)
are missed, and the misses line up with the golden-set failures above. The second round below brought this to
1,267 kept and 2 empty cells (return_eligible x frustrated, delayed x two-in-one); both targets are still missed.

## Second round: v2 data, same recipe

The shortfall above sent the data stage back for a second round (`notes/trajectory-filters/`, "One word, 454
conversations"):

- **Repair.** In 454 rejected conversations the fact-check judge's only objection was a hand-off reply that
  promised a time ("a teammate will reply shortly"). The time words were removed in code (`synth/repair.py`) and
  the 725 conversations that had failed for it were judged again: 327 passed. A `style` check and teacher rule r6
  now stop the habit at the source.
- **Fill.** 479 new conversations aimed at the cells still below two kept (`synth/coverage.py`,
  `synth/generate.py --cells-file`); 106 passed.
- **A coding agent as the teacher.** With the GPU credit used up, a coding agent played Nova and the customer
  through a mailbox that looks like a model server (`synth/agent_server.py`, `synth/agent_generation.md`): same
  tools, database, recording and checks. 12 conversations, 7 passed the code checks, and these 7 were read and
  judged by hand against the same two rubrics. 3 were kept: two failed on tone, and two read "your return is all
  set" for a return only proposed, which `verify.py` now catches.

| | Kept | Empty cells (of 128) | Mix (training examples) |
|---|---|---|---|
| v1 | 1,006 | 28 | 9,485 |
| v2 | **1,267** | **2** | 10,446 (6,115 from kept conversations, 4,129 salvaged leading steps) |

Datasets `nova-trajectories-v2`, `nova-rejects-v2`, `nova-sft2-mix-v2` (private, on the Hub).

SFT-2a was trained again on the v2 mix with the same settings (`training/runs/sft2a-r16-lr2e-4-traj_v2/`,
adapter `123jagjot/sft2a-r16-lr2e-4-traj_v2`). It ran on one L4 on a Google Compute Engine VM, at batch 4 x 4
with gradient checkpointing (one A100 was not available): 653 steps in 84 minutes, peak 5.9 GB. Validation loss
0.514 → 0.217 on a new validation set of 359 examples; probes 5/5 from step 100.

**Results.** Both adapters on one vLLM server on that L4, each on its own database copy; golden set three times
each, the 50 fresh cases once each (`evals/runs/2026-11-25_{golden,fresh}-sft2a_v{1,2}-l4*`). v1 scores 69/107
on the L4 as it did on Modal, so the serving change moved nothing.

| pass@1 % | Order tasks | Policy | Old policy | Returns | Escalation | Unanswerable | Adversarial | All |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Golden, v1 ×3 | 64 | 75 | 60 | 44 | 47 | 62 | 89 | **64** [56–73] |
| Golden, v2 ×3 | 57 | 72 | 67 | 42 | 37 | 60 | 86 | **61** [53–69] |
| Fresh, v1 | 58 | 83 | 100 | 71 | 40 | 43 | 80 | **66** [52–80] |
| Fresh, v2 | 67 | 75 | 100 | 29 | 20 | 43 | 100 | **60** [46–74] |

Grounding issues per 100 replies: 5.4 → 6.3 on golden, 3.9 → 7.8 on fresh (4 replies of 51). No return claimed
done by either.

**v2 is not better.** Every gap is inside the 95% intervals, but golden and fresh point the same way, and the
cases that moved the most are mostly v2's: four golden cases v1 passes in all three runs and v2 in at most one
(esc-004, ord-019, pol-001, adv-009), against one the other way (pol-021). In most of v2's extra misses it takes
the right steps and leaves a fact out of the reply: a hand-off with no delivery date (esc-004, esc-f03), a
return proposal with no refund amount (ret-f01). v2's replies after a hand-off are shorter (25 words on
average against 29, 27 and 28 replies); a likely cause, not tested, is the repair, which removed the time words
and left terser hand-off sentences for the student to copy. pol-001 ("the help center does not
cover" a rule it states) and ret-f05 (asks "Would you like me to proceed?" instead of proposing) are old
failures that v2 hits more often.

**Release gate** (`evals/gate.py`, rules written before any of these runs): v2 against v1 **BLOCKs** on both case
sets. The hard rules fail for both adapters alike: 6 privacy hits on golden and 2 on fresh (the tool-name leak
"call list_my_orders" that `04_comparison.md` found) and adversarial below 100% on golden (v1 32/36, v2 31/36).
v2 adds two blocks of its own: p95 latency 3.12 s on one-tool-call turns (budget 3.0 s; v1 2.81 s, both on a
shared GPU) and fresh grounding +3.9 per 100 (allowed +2). **v1 stays Nova's model**, behind the guardrail layer
`04_comparison.md` decided on.

What did not help is itself a finding: a quarter more kept conversations and 26 more covered cells moved nothing
measurable. The misses that remain (help center misread, hand-offs missing facts, asking instead of acting) need
targeted examples or preference pairs, not more of the same.

## Next

- Teach the hand-offs the golden set expects: a parcel stuck far past its estimate, an angry repeat contact,
  out-of-scope requests (search, then offer a teammate). These are data fixes: more such conversations, and the
  late-parcel filter relaxed for parcels well past their estimate.
- Hand-off replies that carry the facts: the delivery date or the refund amount next to the ticket number. v2
  shows that fixing a wording habit in the data can make replies leave out facts, so a repair needs a re-check of
  what the replies still say.
- The two empty cells and the adversarial cells written for the open-weights teacher
  (`synth/agent_plan_adversarial.json`), when a GPU teacher is available again.
- Preference tuning on the rejects: `nova-rejects-v2` keeps every rejected conversation with its reasons, the
  raw material for chosen/rejected pairs.
